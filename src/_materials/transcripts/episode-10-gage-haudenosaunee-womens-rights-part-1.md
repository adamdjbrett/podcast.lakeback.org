**[09:27] Ciarrai Eaton:** I think where the lake and the natural world comes into the whole discussion is in the matriarchy versus the patriarchy, and I think that that is something that requires a little bit of definition, because when I came into this research, I thought that matriarchy was taking that pyramid of patriarchy and just substituting women as the power, but that's not what it is at all.

**[09:56] Ciarrai Eaton:** In my research, I have found that it's a completely different structure of building a society. **[11:29] Ciarrai Eaton:** So I think to reframe our brains out of this pyramid scheme that we've been raised with and forced into, and to reframe as a circle is a beautiful thing, and I think that's what Matilda saw in the Haudenosaunee.

**[14:50] Ciarrai Eaton:** If she were to have a business or even just a cottage industry out of her house, her husband would get all of the proceeds. He ran the bank account. He ran the house accounts. That happened a lot, where women were doing the work that then men were taking credit for.

**[15:07] Ciarrai Eaton:** A woman could not own a business. A woman could not have a bank account. A woman could not sue in a court of law. She would have to have a man bring a suit for her. **[16:09] Ciarrai Eaton:** It's her clan's house. He has come to live with her, and if he's not doing his job, she could take his stuff and put it outside, and they're divorced. **[16:29] Ciarrai Eaton:** She wrote about it like five or six different times, and I think that the idea that she wrote about it that much shows us how upset she was, that if she wanted to leave her husband, she couldn't take her children with her.

**[16:42] Ciarrai Eaton:** She had no claim over the house that was theirs. She had no claim over her books or anything that she had gathered through her lifetime. **[17:13] Ciarrai Eaton:** For some women, they wanted the right to vote, and that was the end of it. But for Matilda, she was always looking for equal rights. She wanted rights for all people.
