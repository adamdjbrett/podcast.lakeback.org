# Episode 04: Rex Lyons on Lacrosse, the Creator's Game, and the Haudenosaunee Nationals, Part 1

## Introduction

**[00:00] Adam DJ Brett:** Imagine Central New York without lacrosse. You can't. The game has been played on this land since time immemorial. The Onondaga Nation calls it Deyhontsigwa’ehs, or "they bump hips." In 1636, a Jesuit missionary watched it, judged it too violent, and gave it a new French name. Two centuries later, settlers in Canada took it up. They changed the rules, changed the stick, and called it their national game. From there, it travelled into the British Empire and into the Olympics before being removed from the Olympics because the players from Indigenous nations, especially the Haudenosaunee players, were too professional. But now, lacrosse is poised to return to the Olympics.

**[00:50] Adam DJ Brett:** For the Haudenosaunee, it was never only a sport. It is the Creator's Game, or the Medicine Game, a game played for healing and to lift the spirits of the people. It is the Creator's Game, and it is still played in the Sky World. The wooden stick is made from a living hickory tree, and it carries that tree's spirits. Rex Lyons has carried a wooden stick his whole life. He is Onondaga Nation, Eel Clan, and at seventeen, he made the first Iroquois Nationals team. Today, he sits on the board of the Haudenosaunee Nationals, and he is helping to lead the fight for the people who gave the world this game to play under their own flag when lacrosse returns to the Olympics in Los Angeles in 2028. Today, we have Rex Lyons with us to talk about the Creator's Game.
