# Professor's editorial decisions (binding for the revision and verification passes)

These settle the questions the drafters, harvesters and student readers raised. Where a decision conflicts with a draft, the decision wins.

## Form

1. **Host and guest lines.** One line each, short, because full bios sit in Series Details:
   `Host: Betty Hill (Lyons)` · `Guest: Jake Edwards (Onondaga Nation, Eel Clan)` · `Guests: Tina Thomas (Onondaga Nation, Eel Clan) and Sarah Shute`. Give a guest's nation and clan, or one role if they have no nation and clan to give (Joseph J. Heath, General Counsel for the Onondaga Nation; Ciarrai Eaton, Executive Director of the Matilda Joslyn Gage Foundation; Tadodaho Sidney Hill, Tadodaho of the Haudenosaunee Confederacy; Richard W. Hill Sr., Tuscarora Nation, Beaver Clan).
2. **Run time** stays `Run time: TBD`.
3. **Descriptions.** 120 to 200 words (episodes 7, 8 and 9 may reach 240). Do not end every description with the formula "It leaves a class asking…". End on the question itself, stated as a question, and vary how you get there.
4. **Discussion questions.** Exactly five per episode. Each question is at most two sentences and about 60 words. One question, one task: split or cut any question that asks three things. Keep the substance of the three questions from the episode page. A question may point to one named resource, never to "the listed resources" in general.
5. **Additional Resources.** Six to nine per episode, as `- [Title](URL): one-line note.` or, for print sources, a Chicago bibliography entry followed by a one-line note. Order: Onondaga Nation and Haudenosaunee-authored sources first, then primary documents, then scholarship, then journalism. Remove every `GAP:` line from the published text.
6. **No TA notes in the published sections.** Carry unresolved items into the change log instead.

## Wording

7. Write **"land rights action"** in our own prose and in resource notes, including notes copied from the episode pages that say "land claim." Keep a published title exactly as printed.
8. Spell the Great Law's Onondaga name **Gayanashagowa** (as the podcast site does). Spell the Thanksgiving Address **Ganohé:nio**. Write **Two Row Wampum** in prose. Write **Skä•noñh**. Use **Betty Hill (Lyons)** in prose and "Betty Lyons Hill" or "Betty Lyons" only inside a published citation or title that prints it that way.
9. The 2014 filing is a **petition to the Inter-American Commission on Human Rights** (a body of the Organization of American States). Never "World Court," even though some source titles say so; keep such a title only inside a citation.
10. Haudenosaunee Nationals: use the current name, and say "then called the Iroquois Nationals" once where the history needs it.

## Facts and numbers

11. **Legal chronology follows the documents, not memory.** Use these, each of which the verifier is checking against a primary source: land rights action filed March 11, 2005, in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of New York; *City of Sherrill v. Oneida Indian Nation* decided later that month; district court dismissal September 22, 2010; Second Circuit summary order October 19, 2012; U.S. Supreme Court denial of certiorari October 2013; petition to the Inter-American Commission April 14, 2014; the Commission's 2023 decision described in the Onondaga Nation's own words from its July 7, 2023 release. Where a speaker's spoken date differs, the description gives the documented date and does not correct the speaker by name.
12. **Contested figures stay out.** Do not print pounds of mercury, cleanup costs, acreage, numbers of Superfund sub-sites, percentages of contamination remaining, fish-meal counts, or the founding date of the Confederacy. If a figure is essential to what the speaker argues, attribute it plainly ("Heath says that…") and list it in the change log for the professor.
13. **The Two Row's date.** Give none in our prose. Richard Hill says in episode 9 that the date is disputed.
14. **The eagles.** Say "the first bald eagle nest on record at the lake." Do not state how many years had passed.
15. **Unsourced claims from speakers** (the 1911 Albany fire, the Everett report's dates, "Resolution 331," a Supreme Court ruling about a mile around the lake, the suffrage-movement "deal," and similar) stay out of descriptions unless the verification report marks them verified. A question may still ask students to weigh a claim as the speaker's claim.

## Care

16. **Stay inside what the speakers said on air and what the Onondaga Nation publishes.** Describe the Peacemaker narrative only in the outline Tadodaho Sidney Hill and Richard Hill give. Leave out ceremony details, medicine, prophecy and creation-story passages, and the meaning of individual wampum belts beyond the Two Row as Hill tells it.
17. **Never supply an Onondaga name for the lake.** The Nation says it hopes to share that name someday.
18. **Private matters stay out:** a guest's late husband, a family member's illness, the elder who went to the lake, a child's name. The relationship between Betty Hill (Lyons) and Tadodaho Sidney Hill may be mentioned only if it is said on air in episode 6, and then in three words or fewer.
19. **Leave out** Heath's remark connecting a sitting federal judge to another matter, and the name of the private citizen behind the 2011 resolution. Quote Heath's strongest language at most once per episode.
20. **Do not list** the lakeback.org "Original Instructions" page (a full retelling of the Peacemaker story). Where a list includes Rine 2023, also include the Onondaga Nation's own page on the same subject.
21. **Do not excerpt the NOON booklet.** Link to it, and where a specific reading fits, name the reading, its author and its printed pages.
22. **Do not print Gage's Mohawk name** unless the Gage Foundation's own About page prints it; if it does, use that spelling and attribute it to the Foundation.
23. **The influence argument in episodes 10 and 11** is presented as Ciarrai Eaton's and Sally Roesch Wagner's argument. Keep one question per episode that asks what evidence supports it, worded so it tilts students neither toward doubt nor toward acceptance.

## Filling gaps

24. Fill a gap only with a source that has an entry in one of the four ledgers and whose Verification field is not `unverified`. Copy the title, URL and citation from the ledger. If no ledger entry fits, leave the gap unfilled and record it in the change log.
25. Print sources already on an episode page (for example Williams, *Kayanerenkó:wa*; Rodriguez, *A Clan Mother's Call*; Bassett; Noel; Perreault, Wraight and Perreault; Wagner) stay only if the verification report confirms their publication facts. Use the facts the report gives.
