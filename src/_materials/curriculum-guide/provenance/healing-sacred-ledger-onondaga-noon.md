# Source ledger: onondaganation.org and the NOON booklet

Researcher: curriculum-guide project, *Healing the Sacred*. Cluster: the Onondaga Nation's own website and the NOON booklet. All fetches were made November 14, 2026 (the "Accessed" date in every web citation). 45 entries.

## How to read this ledger

- **Nation pages** are cited with the Nation as author unless the page gives a byline. Most Nation pages carry no date on the page itself. For those, **Date** reads `TBD`, followed by the site's own metadata date in parentheses. That metadata date is a posting date only (many read 2014-02, which looks like a site migration). Do not cite it as the date of writing.
- **The Nation's news section mixes the Nation's own releases with reprinted outside journalism** (Post-Standard, Daily Orange, Native News Online, HaudenosauneeNationals.com). Each such entry says who actually wrote it.
- **NOON booklet publication facts, exactly as the booklet's front matter gives them** (title page, PDF p. 2; back cover, PDF p. 82): title *Neighbor to Neighbor, Nation to Nation: Readings About the Relationship of the Onondaga Nation with Central New York USA*; "Published by Neighbors of the Onondaga Nation (NOON)"; "Revised & Expanded 2014". No place of publication is printed (cite as "n.p."). The Introduction (printed p. 6) says it is a revised and expanded version of the booklet NOON published in November 2001, with materials "collected and edited by Carol Baum and Carole Resnick on behalf of NOON," and by Wendy Gonyea, Kent Lyons, Debby Webster and Mary Cook on behalf of the Onondaga Nation. Printed p. 7 says NOON is "a project of the Syracuse Peace Council." File facts (not front matter): 82-page PDF, 8.2 MB, file metadata dated July 30, 2019, OCR text layer (some OCR slips, for example "I65,000" for 165,000).
- **Page numbers.** The booklet's printed page number equals PDF page minus 1 (printed p. 5 is PDF p. 6). Chicago citations use printed pages. Verification fields give PDF pages with printed pages in brackets. The title page, "The Edge of the Woods" and the table of contents carry no printed number.
- **HTTP status** was checked with `curl -L` on every URL below; all returned 200.
- "Episodes" are the numbers 1 to 11 from the brief.

## Cautions

**No page on onondaganation.org that I read is marked "not for general circulation."** The site and the booklet do not carry such a label. The points below are my judgment about what needs the Nation's or NOON's sign-off or a second look.

- **Ceremony and sacred objects.** The Ceremonies page (https://www.onondaganation.org/culture/ceremonies/, 200; read, not given an entry) lists ceremonies and the moon names, and entry 29 describes tobacco burning, the Great Feather Dance and the Thanksgiving Address inside the Longhouse. The Nation published both, but a curriculum guide should link to them rather than excerpt or paraphrase ceremony; ask the Nation before reproducing more than a short quotation. The booklet's repatriation press release (printed p. 37) says wampum are communal property and sacred masks are for traditional use; no photographs of belts or masks should be reproduced without the Nation's permission.
- **The lake's Onondaga name.** The Vision page (entry 33) says the Nation has its own name for the lake and "someday" hopes to share it. Do not supply or guess that name in the curriculum guide.
- **Maple Bay is not covered in this cluster.** I searched the Nation's site search and its crawled sections and found no page on Maple Bay, and the booklet does not mention it. Sources for Episodes 2, 5, 8 and 11 on Maple Bay must come from the aila.ngo and lakeback.org cluster. The 2022 Tully Valley land return (https://www.onondaganation.org/news/2022/one-thousand-acres-returned-to-onondaga-nation/, 200) is a Native News Online reprint and is a different parcel.
- **Eagles on the site.** The Nation's site has no eagle-nest page of its own for the 2022 nest. The two eagle items found (2006, 2009) are reprinted *Post-Standard* journalism. Do not present them as the Nation's voice.
- **Reprints inside the news section.** Also reprinted there: the 2023 HaudenosauneeNationals.com piece on the 2028 Olympics, the Buffalo News piece on Oren Lyons and the Vatican's repudiation of the Doctrine of Discovery, and the Daily Orange piece on restoring Onondaga Creek. If used, cite the original author and outlet (as entry 45 does), not the Nation.
- **Numbers and labels that disagree or are out of date.**
  - Mercury discharged: the booklet's lake page says 185,000 pounds (printed p. 58), its environmental timeline says 165,000 (printed p. 70), and the Nation's Lake page and 2015 statement say 165,000.
  - Daily Solvay waste: eight million pounds (booklet, printed p. 58) versus six million pounds (Nation's Lake page).
  - Corporate defendants: "four corporations" (booklet, printed p. 63) versus "five corporations" on the Land Rights page, which names four.
  - Territory: 7,300 acres (Nation's Facts and Today pages) versus "7,500 acres" in the 2023 release (entry 40), before and after the 2022 land return; verify before stating a current figure.
  - The Hough Report appears under 1778 in the booklet's timeline (printed p. 23) and under 1861 on the Nation's timeline (entry 22).
  - The Land Rights page says "As of 2020, the case has yet to be presented," which the 2022 and 2023 releases supersede.
  - The Land Rights page and its sub-page title call the 2014 filing a suit "in World Court" and "at the United Nations." The petition (entry 37) was filed with the Inter-American Commission on Human Rights of the Organization of American States, not the UN or the International Court of Justice; a 2022 Native News Online reprint repeats the ICJ error. Use the petition's wording.
- **Booklet is a 2014 snapshot.** Pieces such as the hydrofracking chapter say "at time of print"; the 2005 to 2014 case history stops at the Supreme Court denial. Pair with entries 40 and 41 for what followed.
- **Booklet gender statements.** The lacrosse reading (entry 6) and the Nation's Lacrosse page (entry 30) both state that the original game belongs to men. Episode 5 discusses women and lacrosse through Rex Lyons's account; do not let a student read either page as the whole range of Haudenosaunee views, and note the curriculum guide's source for women's lacrosse (the dissertation in Episode 4's list) is outside this cluster.
- **Permissions for the booklet.** NOON offers the PDF as a free download, but individual pieces carry separate copyright notices ("The Edge of the Woods," copyright 1996 Swamp; "Discipline of the Good Mind," Freida J. Jacques; Wagner's pieces originally in *National NOW Times* and elsewhere; photographs by Kent Lyons and others). Linking is safe. Reproducing a chapter or photograph in the guide's print edition needs NOON's written permission (NOON is a project of the Syracuse Peace Council).
- **Terms and spellings.** The booklet says it uses "Iroquois" only where a source text does (for example "Iroquois Nationals"). Nation pages spell the Peacemaker's helper and the first woman convert "Jikonhsaseh," "Hayenhwatha'" and "Tadodaho"/"Tadadaho" variably. For the curriculum guide, follow the episode pages' spellings (for example Jigonsaseh in Episode 11) and quote the Nation's spelling only inside a title or quotation.
- **Personal details in filings.** The Inter-American Commission petition (entry 37) prints a street address and telephone number for the petitioner's representative. Do not reproduce the cover page.
- **Not read.** Joseph Heath's 2006 declaration (https://www.onondaganation.org/mediafiles/pdfs/lgl_alrtsjoedecl.pdf, 200) is an image-only scan with no text layer; I did not read it and did not list it. Episode 7 links to it, so the production team should check that the link and its description are accurate.
- **Instructions inside sources.** None of the pages or PDFs read contained instructions directed at me.
