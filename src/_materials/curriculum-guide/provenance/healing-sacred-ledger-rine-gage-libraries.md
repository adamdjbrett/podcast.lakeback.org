## Top 12 for the series-wide Key Resources list

1. Rine 2023, "Onondaga Lake as Sacred Space and Contested Space" (entry 1): the professor's named author and the best single narrative of the lake, Doctrine of Discovery and cleanup.
2. Rine 2025, "Onondaga Lake in New York State" (entry 2): five pages, assignable whole, covering the Solvay Process, mercury and the 1994 Superfund listing.
3. Bergeron 2017 dissertation (entry 5): the fullest account of land loss, the land rights action and the Superfund process together.
4. Effler and Hennigan 1996 (entry 3): the standard peer-reviewed record of the lake's pollution (text must be pulled from the DOI).
5. Arnold and Bigtree 2025, "The Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) Great Binding Peace" (entry 9): short, current, tied to the Skä•noñh Center.
6. Hill and Coleman 2019 (entry 16): Richard W. Hill Sr.'s own writing on the Two Row and Covenant Chain, the guest of Episode 9.
7. Parmenter 2013, "The Meaning of Kaswentha" (entry 14): shows oral tradition and the documents agree on the Two Row.
8. Miller 2019, "The Doctrine of Discovery" (entry 28): an eight-page, plain overview of the Doctrine for students new to it.
9. Wagner, *Sisters in Spirit* (entry 29): the base text for Haudenosaunee influence on the suffrage movement.
10. Gage, *Woman, Church and State* (entry 30): the primary text for Episodes 10-11, freely available.
11. Kimmerer, *Braiding Sweetgrass* (entry 31): "The Sacred and the Superfund" gives an Indigenous scientist's view of the lake.
12. Matilda Joslyn Gage Center, "About" (entry 33): the only source found that states the 1893 Wolf Clan adoption and the name Karonienhawi.

Runners-up: Fenton 1998 (entry 20) and *Exiled in the Land of the Free* (entry 26) for the Great Law; Oberg 2016 (entry 22) for Canandaigua; the Nation's 2014 petition (entry 12) as a primary legal document.
