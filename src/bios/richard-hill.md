Richard W. Hill Sr. is a citizen of the Beaver Clan of the Tuscarora Nation of the Haudenosaunee at Grand River, and he lives at Six Nations of the Grand River Territory. He is an artist, historian and educator. He was born in Buffalo, New York, to a Mohawk father and a Tuscarora mother, and he holds a master's degree in American Studies from the State University of New York at Buffalo.

His career has moved between museums and universities. He was Assistant Director for Public Programs at the Smithsonian Institution's National Museum of the American Indian and Museum Director at the Institute of American Indian Arts in Santa Fe, New Mexico. He was also an assistant professor of Native American Studies at SUNY Buffalo, where he taught alongside Oren Lyons and John Mohawk, and later taught at McMaster University.

He recently retired as Senior Project Coordinator of the Deyohahá:ge: Indigenous Knowledge Centre at Six Nations Polytechnic in Ohsweken, Ontario. He now serves as Indigenous Innovations Specialist at Mohawk College and as a cultural advisor at FNTI, Tyendinaga Mohawk Territory. He is also working as an interpretive specialist on exhibitions for the renovated Mohawk Institute, the oldest Indian residential school in Canada.

In his account, a job at a historical museum in Buffalo first brought him to the wampum belts held in collections. He has since helped recover wampum from universities and museums. He speaks on the Great Law, the Two Row Wampum and the Covenant Chain.
