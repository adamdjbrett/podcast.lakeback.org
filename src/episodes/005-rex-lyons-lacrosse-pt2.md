## Show notes

Gaeñ hia uh, Betty Lyons Hill, Executive Director of the American Indian Law Alliance, continues her conversation with De Ho Wih Da Dis (Rex Lyons) about the cultural, political, and environmental significance of Haudenosaunee lacrosse. The discussion explores the responsibilities associated with representing the Haudenosaunee Nationals, the importance of cultural identity and intergenerational knowledge, and ongoing efforts to achieve international recognition and Olympic inclusion. Through reflections in the episode, the relationship between land governance, environmental stewardship, and protection of the sacred site of Onondaga Lake is examined.

## Discussion questions

1. How does De Ho Wih Da Dis describe the responsibilities associated with being a member of the Haudenosaunee Nationals? How does this perspective challenge the conventional “Western” understandings of athletic achievement and individual success?
2. What connections does this discussion establish between Haudenosaunee governance, cultural identity, and environmental stewardship? How might these perspectives inform contemporary approaches to leadership and sustainability?
3. Why is the campaign for the protection and reclamation of Onondaga Lake significant to Haudenosaunee communities? How does this campaign illustrate the relationship between environmental justice, cultural preservation, and the matter of Indigenous sovereignty?
