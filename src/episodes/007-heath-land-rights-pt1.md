## Show notes

In this episode, Gaeñ hia uh, Betty Lyons Hill, Executive Director of the American Indian Law Alliance, interviews Joseph J. Heath, General Counsel for the Onondaga Nation, about his decades of legal advocacy and work supporting Haudenosaunee sovereignty. Heath discusses his early experiences concerning the Attica prison rebellion, his relationships with Onondaga leaders, and the cultural teachings that shape his understandings of Indigenous sovereignty. The conversation examines the [Onondaga Land Rights Action](https://www.onondaganation.org/land-rights/), the dismissal of Indigenous land rights cases in US courts, and the Nation’s ongoing efforts to pursue justice through international human rights mechanisms. Through reflections on treaty rights, environmental protections, diplomacy, and cultural responsibility, the episode highlights the challenges of achieving Indigenous land justice and the importance of continued advocacy.

## Discussion questions

1. How did Heath’s relationships with Onondaga elders shape his understanding of Indigenous sovereignty? How might this differ from “western” or conventional understandings of sovereignty within the US legal system?
2. Why did the Onondaga Nation choose to pursue a [Land Rights Action](https://www.onondaganation.org/land-rights/) rather than a traditional land claim? What does this distinction reveal about the Nation’s goals regarding healing, environmental protections, and relationships with neighboring communities?
3. What challenges did the Onondaga Nation encounter when pursuing justice through the US courts, and why did it turn to international human rights mechanisms? What does this case illustrate about the relationship between Indigenous treaty rights, legal systems, and political power?

## Further resources

- [Neighbor to Neighbor, Nation to Nation](https://peacecouncil.net/sites/default/files/NOON-booklet-web.pdf): Free educational booklet (PDF) of readings about the relationship between the Onondaga Nation and Central New York.
- [Court denies Onondaga land rights lawsuit](https://aila.ngo/court-denies-onondaga-land-rights-lawsuit/): News report from 2010 on the federal dismissal of the Onondaga Nation's 2005 Land Rights Action under Sherrill.
- [City of Sherrill v. Oneida Indian Nation, 544 U.S. 197 (2005)](https://doctrineofdiscovery.org/blog/sherrill-v-oneida-doctrine-christian-discovery-denial-treaty-rights/): Commentary on the Supreme Court decision issued weeks after the Land Rights Action was filed and how it relies on the Doctrine of Discovery.
- [City of Sherrill v. Oneida Indian Nation of New York](https://doctrineofdiscovery.org/sherrill-v-oneida-opinion-of-the-court/): Dana Lloyd's summary of the decision, with excerpts and a PDF of the Court's opinion for classroom reading.
- [Onondaga Nation v. NY](https://doctrineofdiscovery.org/onondaga-nation-v-ny/): The Second Circuit appeal that upheld the dismissal on the equitable grounds set out in Sherrill.
- [The Doctrine of Christian Discovery and Domination (Joseph J. Heath)](https://jcrt.org/archives/25.1/heath/): Heath's own article in the *Journal for Cultural and Religious Theory* 25, no. 1 (Spring 2026): 51–60, on how U.S. courts used Discovery to dismiss Haudenosaunee land rights cases.
- Speak, Larissa. ["The Onondaga Nation's Land Claim: Rights Without a Remedy?"](https://digitalcommons.law.seattleu.edu/ailj/vol13/iss2/2) *American Indian Law Journal* 13, no. 2 (2025). Legal analysis of the Marshall trilogy, Sherrill, and international law as they bear on the Onondaga Nation's [land rights action](https://www.onondaganation.org/land-rights/).
- Bergeron, Emily. "From Sovereignty to Superfund: The Onondaga Nation's Legal Battle for Land Rights, Environmental Justice, and the Remediation of Onondaga Lake." PhD diss., Cornell University, 2017. Long study connecting the land rights action to the lake cleanup.
