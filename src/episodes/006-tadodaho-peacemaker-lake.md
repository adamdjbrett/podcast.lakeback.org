## Show notes

In this episode, Gaeñ hia uh, Betty Lyons Hill, Executive Director of the American Indian Law Alliance, interviews Tadodaho Sidney Hill about Haudenosaunee history, governance, and cultural responsibilities. The conversation touches on the Great Law of Peace, the role of Tadodaho, the significance of Onondaga Lake as a sacred site, and the intersection of Haudenosaunee identity and Confederacy history. Together, they reflect on environmental contamination, land rights, cultural knowledge lost through displacement, environmental justice, and the importance of restoring connections between Haudenosaunee children, their ancestors, and the natural world.

## Discussion questions

1. How does Tadodaho Hill explain the historical and cultural significance of the Great Law of Peace and the role of the Tadodaho, and what can this teach us about Indigenous approaches to leadership and community governance?
2. Why is Onondaga Lake important to Haudenosaunee history and identity beyond its physical or recreational value? How does its contamination illustrate the relationship between environmental injustices and cultural losses?
3. Tadodaho Hill discusses the difference between doing what is legally required and doing what is right. How does this distinction apply to environmental protection and the reclamation of Indigenous lands? What responsibilities do governments, communities, and individuals have in addressing these issues?
