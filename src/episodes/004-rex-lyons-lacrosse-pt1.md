## Show notes

In this episode, Gaeñ hia uh, Betty Lyons Hill, Executive Director of the American Indian Law Alliance, interviews De Ho Wih Da Dis (Rex Lyons), Onondaga Nation, Eel Clan, about the cultural, spiritual, and historical significance of lacrosse within Haudenosaunee communities. The discussion explores the history of lacrosse as a medicine game, its connection to Indigenous traditions and environmental stewardship, and the importance of maintaining relationships with the natural world. The Haudenosaunee Nationals are also examined as an expression of sovereignty, self-determination, and cultural identity. Through personal experiences and reflections, the episode highlights the role of lacrosse in cultural preservation and intergenerational knowledge.

## Discussion questions

1. How does De Ho Wih Da Dis (Rex Lyons) describe the cultural and spiritual significance of lacrosse within Haudenosaunee communities, and how does this understanding differ from viewing lacrosse as merely a competitive sport?
2. The episode emphasizes the importance of maintaining relationships with the natural world. How does this perspective challenge dominant Western ideas and approaches to education, technology, and environmental responsibility?
3. What is the significance of the Haudenosaunee Nationals in relation to self-determination, Indigenous sovereignty, and cultural identity? How does the development of the team illustrate the relationship between sports and cultural preservation?
