## Show notes

In this episode, Patricia Nolan Vogt continues the conversation with Ciarrai Eaton, Executive Director of the Matilda Joslyn Gage Foundation, and explores Matilda Joslyn Gage’s relationship with the Haudenosaunee and the influence of Haudenosaunee women’s roles and governance on her advocacy for equal rights. The discussion examines Gage’s adoption into the Mohawk Wolf Clan, her efforts to educate white communities about Haudenosaunee sovereignty, and her criticism of broken treaties and unequal treatment of Indigenous nations. It also considers how Haudenosaunee understandings of women’s leadership, nature, and the protection of life influenced Gage and other women involved in the women’s rights movement. Finally, the conversation highlights the importance of recovering overlooked historical narratives and recognizing the connections between Indigenous sovereignty, environmental protection, and ongoing struggles for justice.

## Discussion questions

1. How did Gage’s relationship with the Haudenosaunee influence her views on women’s rights and Indigenous sovereignty?
2. What differences does the episode describe between Haudenosaunee perspectives on women and the views of the dominant Christian settler society during Gage’s lifetime?
3. Why is it important to recognize and preserve the stories of people who have been overlooked or erased from historical narratives?

- Noel, Jan. “‘Fertile with Fine Talk’: Ungoverned Tongues among Haudenosaunee Women and Their Neighbors.” *Ethnohistory* 57, no. 2 (2010). Examines the authority Haudenosaunee women held in governance and the colonial records that describe it.
