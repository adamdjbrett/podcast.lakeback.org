## Show notes

In this episode, Patricia Nolan Vogt, Tina Thomas, and Sarah Shute continue to explore the cultural and spiritual significance of the bald eagle within Haudenosaunee teachings, particularly its role as a guardian of peace under the Great Law of Peace. They also discuss their efforts to protect the eagles nesting at the Skä•noñh Center, the importance of returning ancestral lands such as Maple Bay to the Onondaga Nation, and the need to recognize Indigenous relationships with the natural world. The conversation also examines the [Thanksgiving Address](https://americanindian.si.edu/environment/pdf/01_02_Thanksgiving_Address.pdf) (Ganohé:nio), or the words that come before all else, as a daily practice of gratitude, connection, and responsibility toward all living beings. Finally, Thomas and Shute emphasize the importance of learning Indigenous histories, slowing down to observe the natural environment, and educating future generations about the relationships that sustain life and support peace.

## Discussion questions

1. What does the eagle represent in the Great Law of Peace, and how does its role as a guardian connect to Tina Thomas and Sarah Shute’s efforts to protect the eagles at the Skä•noñh Center?
2. How do Tina and Sarah describe the Thanksgiving Address as a way of building relationships with the natural world? What are some ways people might incorporate these teachings into their daily lives?
3. Why do Tina and Sarah emphasize learning the Indigenous history of the places where we live? How might developing a relationship with the land change the way people treat their environment?
