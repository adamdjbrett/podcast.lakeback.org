## Show notes

In this episode, Patricia Nolan Vogt speaks with Ciarrai Eaton, Executive Director of the Matilda Joslyn Gage Foundation, about the connections between Haudenosaunee society and the women’s rights movement. Eaton discusses how Matilda Joslyn Gage drew inspiration from Haudenosaunee systems of governance, particularly the influence and authority of women, matrilineal family structures, and the cultural respect afforded to women’s knowledge. The conversation examines the differences between Haudenosaunee social structures and the legal restrictions imposed on women through the settler system of coverture. Eaton also explores the importance of oral histories, the historical erasure of women’s contributions, and the argument that Haudenosaunee women helped influence early suffragists. Throughout the episode, the speakers consider how recognizing Indigenous women’s political and cultural contributions can broaden our understanding of the history of democracy and women’s rights.

## Discussion questions

1. How did Haudenosaunee systems of governance and social organization influence Matilda Joslyn Gage’s understanding of women’s rights and equality? What aspects of Haudenosaunee society challenged the assumptions of the settler society in which she lived?
2. What is the difference between matriarchy and matrilineal descent, as explained in the episode? How do these concepts help us understand the roles of women in Haudenosaunee society?
3. Why does Eaton emphasize the importance of oral histories and the recovery of overlooked historical narratives? How might recognizing Haudenosaunee women’s influence change the way we understand the history of the women’s rights movement?

- [Matilda Joslyn Gage Foundation](https://www.matildajoslyngage.org/): The foundation Ciarrai Eaton leads, with information on Gage's life, her home in Fayetteville, and its programs.
- [Haudenosaunee women inspired women’s suffrage movement](https://aila.ngo/haudenosaunee-women-inspired-womens-suffrage-movement/): 2020 commentary by Betty Lyons on Haudenosaunee women’s influence on the suffrage movement.
- [NY Daily News Op-Ed: Haudenosaunee Influence on Women’s Rights Movement](https://aila.ngo/ny-daily-news-op-ed-haudenosaunee-influence-on-womens-rights-movement/): 2020 post linking to an op-ed by Betty Lyons and Sally Roesch Wagner on overlooked suffrage figures and matrilineal Haudenosaunee society.
- Wagner, Sally Roesch. *Sisters in Spirit: Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) Influence on Early American Feminists*. Native Voices, 2001. Argues that early American feminists drew on Haudenosaunee women’s roles, the central subject of the episode.
- Noel, Jan. “‘Fertile with Fine Talk’: Ungoverned Tongues among Haudenosaunee Women and Their Neighbors.” *Ethnohistory* 57, no. 2 (2010). Uses colonial-era records to examine the public voice Haudenosaunee women had in governance.
- Rodriguez, Jeanette, with Iakoiane Wakerahkats:teh. *A Clan Mother’s Call: Reconstructing Haudenosaunee Cultural Memory*. State University of New York Press, 2017. Covers the role of clan mothers in Haudenosaunee life, relevant to the episode’s discussion of matrilineal clans.
- Williams, Kayanesenh Paul. *Kayanerenkó:wa: The Great Law of Peace*. University of Manitoba Press, 2018. Background on the Great Law and the clan mothers’ role in choosing leaders.
