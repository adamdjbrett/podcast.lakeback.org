## Show notes

In this episode, Patricia Nolan Vogt explores the personal and cultural connections that Tina Thomas and Sarah Shute have developed with Onondaga Lake through their work at the Skä•noñh Great Law of Peace Center. The discussion examines the lake’s history of environmental contamination, the effects of pollution on wildlife, and the importance of understanding its significance to the Haudenosaunee Confederacy as the birthplace of democracy. Thomas and Shute also describe how the [Thanksgiving Address](https://americanindian.si.edu/environment/pdf/01_02_Thanksgiving_Address.pdf) (Ganohé:nio), the Great Law of Peace, and the return of bald eagles to the lake demonstrate the relationship between cultural teachings, environmental stewardship, and resilience. Their experiences highlight the importance of protecting the lake, respecting the wildlife that depends on it, and sharing Haudenosaunee history and perspectives with the broader community.

## Discussion questions

1. How did Tina Thomas and Sarah Shute’s understanding of Onondaga Lake change as they learned more about its cultural and environmental significance?
2. What does the return of bald eagles to Onondaga Lake represent to Thomas and Shute, and what concerns remain about the lake’s environmental health?
3. How does the Skä•noñh Center use the history of Onondaga Lake and the Great Law of Peace to teach visitors about cooperation, peace, and environmental responsibility?

## Further resources

- [Thanksgiving Address: Greetings to the Natural World](https://americanindian.si.edu/environment/pdf/01_02_Thanksgiving_Address.pdf): PDF from the Smithsonian's National Museum of the American Indian with the words of the Thanksgiving Address in English, for reading alongside the episode.
- [Sean Kirst: At a sacred place, 'mindblowing' tale of the nesting eagles of Onondaga Lake](https://centralcurrent.org/sean-kirst-at-a-sacred-place-mindblowing-tale-of-the-nesting-eagles-of-onondaga-lake/): Central Current column on the eagles nesting at the Skä•noñh Center and the people who watch over them.
- [Saving a Sacred Lake: A Century of Pollution Haunts the Haudenosaunee](https://aila.ngo/saving-a-sacred-lake-a-century-of-pollution-haunts-the-haudenosaunee/): Background on the pollution history that the guests describe.
- [The Cultural Landscape of Onondaga Lake](https://onondagalakehistoricalecology.weebly.com/the-cultural-landscape-of-onondaga-lake.html): Introduction to the lake's former wetlands and uplands and the Onondaga economy that drew on them.
- [Historical Ecology of Onondaga Lake: Timeline](https://onondagalakehistoricalecology.weebly.com/timeline.html): Dated sequence from early habitation through salt production and the state's 1795 acquisition of the lake.
- Rine, Holly A. "Onondaga Lake in New York State: Seventeenth Century Origins of a Superfund Site." *Environment and History* 31(3), 2025. Short article that links the Great Law, colonization, industrial mercury discharge, and the 2022 eagle nest.
- Effler, S. W., and R. D. Hennigan. "Onondaga Lake, New York: Legacy of Pollution." *Lake and Reservoir Management* 12(1), 1996. Scientific overview of the lake's contamination.
- Williams, Kayanesenh Paul. *Kayanerenkó:wa: The Great Law of Peace*. University of Manitoba Press, 2018. Book-length account of the Great Law of Peace.

### From related projects
