## Show notes

In this episode, Gaeñ hia uh, Betty Lyons Hill, Executive Director of the American Indian Law Alliance, continues her discussion with Joseph J. Heath, General Counsel for the Onondaga Nation, about the ongoing efforts to reclaim and protect Onondaga Lake. Heath examines the history of land-return initiatives, the limitations of the Superfund cleanup, and the continuing environmental and public health consequences of pollution. The conversation explores the relationship between environmental justice, Indigenous sovereignty, and the Nation’s efforts to secure meaningful participation in decisions affecting its sacred lands and waters. Heath also discusses how community members can support the Onondaga Nation through advocacy, education, and continued public pressure for accountability and justice.

## Discussion questions

1. Why is Onondaga Lake significant to the cultural, spiritual, and historical identity of the Haudenosaunee? How does its importance extend beyond recreation or property ownership?
2. According to Heath, what are the limitations of the Superfund cleanup, and how have ongoing environmental problems affected surrounding communities? How does this illustrate the relationship between environmental justice and public health?
3. What actions does Heath encourage members of the public to take in support of the Onondaga Nation’s efforts? How can community advocacy, education, and political accountability contribute to Indigenous land rights and environmental justice?

### From the Onondaga Nation

- [Onondaga Lake](https://www.onondaganation.org/land-rights/onondaga-lake/): Overview of the lake's size, depth, tributaries, and pollution history, with a link to a timeline of pollution.
- [Onondaga Lake As A Superfund Site](https://www.onondaganation.org/land-rights/onondaga-lake/onondaga-lake-as-a-superfund-site/): Explains the 1994 federal Superfund listing, the nine sub-sites, and Honeywell's responsibility for most of the cleanup.
- [Contaminants of Concern](https://www.onondaganation.org/land-rights/contaminants-of-concern/): Describes mercury and other contaminants found in the lake-bottom sediments and how they move through the environment.
- [The Cleanup Plan](https://www.onondaganation.org/land-rights/the-cleanup-plan/): Summarizes the state-ordered Honeywell cleanup under the Record of Decision, including planned dredging volumes and capping.
- [Honeywell & Onondaga Lake: A Timeline](https://www.onondaganation.org/land-rights/the-offenders/honeywell-onondaga-lake-a-timeline/): Year-by-year history from 1881 onward of the Solvay and Honeywell operations and waste dumping at the lake.
- [Honeywell ceases dredging of pollutants in sacred Lake](https://www.onondaganation.org/news/2015/honeywell-ceases-dredging-of-pollutants-in-sacred-lake/): January 2015 statement on Honeywell halting lake-bottom dredging, framed around the Nation's stewardship of the lake and the Canandaigua Treaty.
